Paper 1 (fiction) has been well covered. Paper 2 (non-fiction) has not been touched yet — this is the priority catch-up item.

### Source A — Newspaper opinion column
> *It is time schools accepted what most teachers already know: a smartphone in a blazer pocket is a permanent invitation to disengage. Every study on classroom attention tells the same story — a notification, even unread, is enough to fracture concentration for several minutes. We do not allow pupils to bring televisions into lessons. Why, then, do we tolerate a device infinitely more distracting, simply because it is smaller? A total ban, enforced from the school gate, is not old-fashioned. It is the only serious response to a problem we have spent a decade pretending doesn't exist.*

### Source B — Student blog post
> *My phone got confiscated last week for buzzing in my bag — not even out, just buzzing — and I've been thinking about it since. Yes, some people mess about on their phones in lessons. But plenty of us use them properly: checking homework on the school app, translating a word in French, messaging a parent about a late pickup. Banning phones outright punishes everyone for what a few people do. A better answer is teaching pupils to manage their own attention, because that's a skill they'll need long after they've left school and no one is confiscating anything.*

**Task 1 — Q2 Summary (8 marks):** Summarise the differences between Source A and Source B's views on phones in school. Identify 4 clear points of difference, one piece of evidence from each source per point. Continuous prose, not bullet points.

**Task 2 — Q3 Language Analysis (12 marks):** How does the writer of Source A use language to persuade the reader? Identify 3 persuasive devices (rhetorical question, direct comparison, dismissive phrasing, etc.), quote, and analyse effect.

**Task 3 — Q4 Comparison of Perspectives (16 marks):** Compare how the two writers present their attitudes to phones in school. Use a comparative frame throughout (not "Source A then Source B") — comparative connectives: *similarly · in contrast · whereas · while*.

**Task 4 — Q5 Viewpoint Writing (40 marks, 30 min timed):** Write a **letter to your school newspaper** giving your view on: *"Should Year 10 students be allowed a later school start time?"* Structure: strong opening → 3–4 paragraphs, one argument each → acknowledge and rebut a counter-argument → strong conclusion.

## Maths — Circle Theorems, Sine/Cosine Rule, Simultaneous Equations

### Circle Theorems (8 to know)
1. Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference (same arc)
2. Angles in the same segment are equal
3. Angle in a semicircle = 90°
4. Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°
5. Tangent meets radius at 90°
6. Two tangents from an external point are equal in length
7. Alternate segment theorem
8. Perpendicular from centre to chord bisects the chord

**Practice** (name the theorem you use for each):
1. A triangle is drawn with all three vertices on a circle, and one side is the diameter. Find the angle opposite the diameter.
2. Two tangents are drawn from an external point P to a circle, touching at A and B. If PA = 7 cm, state the length of PB and name the theorem used.

### Sine and Cosine Rule (Y10 preview)
Sine Rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C · Cosine Rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A

**Practice:**
1. Triangle with a = 8 cm, angle A = 40°, angle B = 65°. Find side b.
2. Triangle with sides b = 6 cm, c = 9 cm, and included angle A = 55°. Find side a.

### Simultaneous Equations (algebraic + graphical)
**Practice:**

2. Solve algebraically: 3x + 2y = 16, x + 2y = 8. x = 8 y = -4

## Physics — Motion Graphs (Y9 recap)

- **Distance-time graph:** gradient = speed. Flat section = stationary.
- **Velocity-time graph:** gradient = acceleration. Area under graph = distance travelled.

**Exam question:**
> A car accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 8 seconds, travels at a constant 20 m/s for 15 seconds, then decelerates to rest in 5 seconds.
>
> (a) Sketch (describe) the velocity-time graph for this journey. [2]
> (b) Calculate the acceleration during the first 8 seconds. [2]
> (c) Calculate the total distance travelled over the whole 28 seconds (use area under the graph). [3]

## Biology — Respiration and Excretion (Y10 preview)

**Aerobic:** C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP. **Anaerobic (animals):** glucose → lactic acid (produces oxygen debt).

**Excretion organs:** lungs (CO₂, water vapour) · liver (urea from deamination) · kidneys (urea, excess water, ions).

**Task:**
1. State two differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration in animals.
2. Explain why muscles produce lactic acid during intense exercise, and why heavy breathing continues afterwards.
3. Describe, in order, what happens to blood as it passes through a kidney nephron (filtration → selective reabsorption).

## Computing — Sorting Algorithms & Programming Concepts

**Bubble sort:** compare adjacent pairs, swap if out of order, repeat passes until no swaps needed.

**Task:** Trace a bubble sort on this list, showing each pass: `[5, 2, 9, 1, 6]`

**Programming concepts:** trace this pseudocode and state the final output:
