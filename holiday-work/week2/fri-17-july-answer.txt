---------------------------------------------------------

Part A:
1. Enzyme definition:
Enzymes attack to a substrate and break it down, helping speed up the breaking down of chemicals into products 
There is less movement so the substrate is broken down slower 

2. Lock and key model:
the lock is the shape of the active site in the substrate is the key, the lock an key makes it so the substrate can only bind to one type of enzyme
3. Active site / poor fit:
the active site is an area of the enzyme where the substrate binds to the enzyme and breaks it down into its products 

Part B:
1. Shape of rate vs temperature graph:
the rate of reaction initially increases as the temperature increases until the optimum temperature has been reached. after the optimum temperature, the rate of reaction slows down until it reaches 0 where the nezyme has fully been denatured
2. Below optimum:
there is lesser kinetic energy compared to the optimum temperature, so there are fewer particle collisions between molecules, causin the substrate to be broken down slower
3. Above optimum (denature):
the bonds holding the enzymes together break, causing the enzyme to denature. this means the shape of the active site has changed so the substrate can't bind to the enzyme enemy and the lock and key mechanism fails, the enzyme no longer works
4. Pepsin (low pH) vs intestinal enzymes (higher pH):
the enviroment in the stomach is of a low pH, so pepsin works best in this enviroment. in the small intestine, the environment has a higher pH, so the enzymes in the small intestine work best in a higher pH
