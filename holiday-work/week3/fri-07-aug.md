## Session 1 — English · 45 min · Language Paper 1, Q5 Narrative Writing

**Today's focus: narrative writing (the descriptive side was covered on 17 Jul — this is narrative)**

Narrative structure: opening → build-up → complication → climax → resolution. Show character through action and dialogue, not description. Control pace: slow down for key moments, speed up for action.

### Stimulus (2 min)

> Write a story that begins with the sentence: *"The train doors closed before she could reach them."*

### Plan (10 min)

- Sketch the arc: opening → build-up → complication → climax → resolution
- Decide on **one** central complication (what goes wrong, and why it matters to this character specifically)
- Plan your final line before you start writing the middle

### Write your response (30 min, timed)

*(Aim for 350–450 words. Use dialogue sparingly but purposefully. Avoid clichés — no "heart pounding" or "time seemed to stop.")*

## Session 2 — Physics · 45 min · Magnetism and Electromagnetism (Y10 Preview)

### Part A — Permanent Magnets and Electromagnets (10 min)

1. State the rule for how magnetic field lines run around a bar magnet (direction).
2. Describe what a solenoid is and why it behaves like a bar magnet when current flows through it.
3. State **one** advantage an electromagnet has over a permanent magnet.

### Part B — Motor Effect (15 min)

F = BIL (force = flux density × current × length) · Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: Thumb = Force, First finger = Field, Second finger = Current

1. A wire of length 0.4 m carries a current of 3 A in a magnetic field of flux density 0.5 T. Calculate the force on the wire.
2. A current-carrying wire experiences a force of 0.6 N in a field of flux density 0.8 T. The wire is 0.5 m long. Calculate the current.
3. Using Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, describe how you would find the direction of the force if you know the directions of the current and the field.

### Part C — Exam Question: Induction and Transformers (20 min)

> A step-up transformer has 200 turns on the primary coil and 1,000 turns on the secondary coil. The primary voltage is 24 V.
>
> (a) Calculate the secondary voltage. [2]
> (b) State whether this is a step-up or step-down transformer, and explain how you know from the number of turns. [2]
> (c) Explain, in terms of electromagnetic induction, how a transformer transfers energy from the primary to the secondary coil despite there being no direct electrical connection between them. [4]
